# Independent Numerov solver

The Rust static library exposes a versioned, caller-owned C ABI. It solves the physical radial equation at supplied Q, with its own potential implementation. The inner boundary is a constant-potential allowed lead beginning at the supplied lead radius; its incident flux is matched to a unit outgoing Coulomb wave. The result is transmission through that specified open system, not a resonance width. Lead-radius sensitivity is a model assumption and should be examined. No fitted preformation is introduced; if converted to a lifetime, use the same explicit assault frequency and preformation factor as the WKB comparison.

The outgoing wave uses the inverse-radius asymptotic expansion (DLMF 33.11); it is rejected when the expansion does not reach the requested precision. The solver compares steps h, h/2, h/4 and repeats the outer match at twice the requested radius; status is converged only when both differences are at most 0.0001 decades. Numerical failure and nonconvergence are explicit statuses. Square-well jumps can converge slowly on the grid. The numerical implementation has analytic free-wave, potential-step and rectangular-barrier fixtures; the normal barrier fixture has 1e-5-decade tolerance, and the extreme log-transmission fixture uses 0.01 decades to exercise rescaling below floating-point probability range. Coulomb propagation is also checked against three 80-digit mpmath F and G references, including angular momentum and deep tunneling; tests/coulomb_reference.py records the reference construction and DLMF normalization. Do not interpret agreement with WKB as a required identity.

The native uncertainty sampler propagates the supplied Gaussian covariance, or treats supplied standard deviations as independent when covariance is absent and marks that assumption. It reports accepted draws and log-half-life quantiles; rejected draws can bias the conditional distribution. Its output covers the supplied parameter spread only; it does not infer experimental uncertainties or include numerical and model uncertainty.
