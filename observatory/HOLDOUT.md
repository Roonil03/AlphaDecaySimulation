# Ren half-life-fit holdout

`python -m observatory --validate-holdout` reports two channels separately from the unchanged 13-channel `--validate` benchmark. [holdout-validation.json](holdout-validation.json) records a run against the local gateway. Errors are log10(predicted partial half-life / reference partial half-life). For two channels, baseline, Qi and shell MAE are 0.320, 0.397 and 0.180; RMSE are 0.320, 0.484 and 0.186 log10 seconds, respectively. This tiny, selected sample is not evidence of general performance.

[Ren et al.](https://cpc.ihep.ac.cn/article/doi/10.1088/1674-1137/ae167b), introduction, say their 550-point half-life fit comprises 539 NUBASE2020 entries plus 11 named later additions, with experimental uncertainties required to be below 50%. Neither isotope below is among the additional 11. Their NUBASE2020 half-life uncertainties fail that strict criterion. This is a holdout of **Ren's half-life fit**, conditional on the published selection; it does not establish independence from Qi's fit, the liquid-drop binding-energy fit, or other empirical information. Both Q and measured parent mass are inputs, potentially correlated through nuclear data evaluations. Ren's paper does not publish a full list of its 539 entries.

* Dy-154: [NUBASE2020](https://www-nds.iaea.org/amdc/ame2020/NUBASE2020.pdf) gives 3.0 ± 1.5 million years, exactly 50%. The reference used here is the later direct measurement of 1.40 ± 0.08 million years ([measurement](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-12684-6)). [ENSDF](https://www.nndc.bnl.gov/ensnds/150/Gd/a_decay.pdf) identifies a 0+ to 0+ ground-state line and only one observed alpha group; its 100% ground-state fraction is an approximation. Q = 2.94511 ± 0.00495 MeV is total decay energy, not emitted alpha kinetic energy; the latter is about 2.870 MeV. Q and its uncertainty are from [Cai et al. Table VII](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevC.101.054304).
* Os-186: NUBASE2020 gives 2.0 ± 1.1 quadrillion years, a 55% uncertainty, and 100% alpha decay. Cai Table VII lists the 0+ to 0+ ground-state channel and Q = 2.82120 ± 0.00087 MeV. The ground-state branch is approximated as 100%; [Belli et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01508) bound the partial half-life to the first excited state above 3.3 × 10^17 years at 90% confidence. The uncertainty in the measured total half-life is large.

Both binding energies use the direct [AME2020 binding-energy-per-nucleon column](https://www-nds.iaea.org/amdc/ame2020/mass_1.mas20.txt), consistently with the original benchmark. The unflagged Dy-154 and Os-186 rows give 8193.1305 ± 0.0483 and 7982.8324 ± 0.0041 keV/nucleon, respectively. Multiplying the values and uncertainties by A/1000 gives total parent binding energies of 1261.742097 ± 0.0074382 and 1484.8068264 ± 0.0007626 MeV. The half-lives use 365.25 days per year. The uncertainty in half-life, Q, branch fractions and binding energy is recorded or described above but is not propagated into the errors or metrics. Qi's published fit covers Z = 78–108, so both cases are extrapolations. WKB parameters are illustrative, and relative model error may reverse for other nuclei.
